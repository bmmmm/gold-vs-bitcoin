🥇 Gold runs out first — by about a century.

Known gold reserves are exhausted around 2046. The last satoshi won't be mined until ~2140.

Why the last 5% takes a century

Gold leaves the ground at a steady pace — a straight line to zero. Bitcoin's issuance halves every ~4 years, so what's left shrinks in a staircase that never quite reaches the floor.

Plot twist: gold never dies — bitcoin does

🥇 Nothing is ever lost

Gold doesn't get used up — it gets melted down and comes back. Roughly 1,400 t per year re-enters supply through recycling, about a quarter of everything sold.

Virtually all ~219,500 t ever mined still exist above ground — in vaults, jewelry, and circuit boards.

₿ Only losses are forever

Bitcoin can't be melted back. An estimated 3–4 million BTC are lost for good — discarded drives, forgotten keys, and Satoshi's untouched ~1M coins.

The effective cap is likely below 18 million — and it only ever shrinks.

The last gram of gold will never be gone. Millions of bitcoin already are.

All of it, in one cube

Gold is so dense (19.3 t/m³) that everything humanity has ever mined fits in a cube about 22.5 m on a side — it would hide behind an apartment block. The remaining reserves add a 15 m cube.

* "Runs out" means: known economic reserves ÷ current mine production. That number is a moving target — between the 2025 and 2026 USGS editions, world reserves went up from 64,000 to 66,000 t even as ~3,300 t were mined, because new deposits keep being found and reclassified. Bitcoin's finish line, by contrast, is fixed in the protocol.

* Bitcoin is already ~95% mined, yet the last 5% takes another century — the block reward halves every 210,000 blocks, so issuance approaches zero asymptotically.
